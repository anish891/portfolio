Most of this site is about things I have built. This post is about the person who built them, so it is going to wander a little.

## The short version

I am Anish. I live in Mumbai, India. I build software, mostly tools that find the signal in messy data: option chains, spreadsheets, security graphs. I am more of a maker than a specialist, and I would rather be good at the whole path from an idea to something working than expert in one narrow corner.

## What I am like

A few things are true about me however I describe them:

- I like making things. If something does not exist yet, or exists in a way I do not like, my first instinct is to build it.
- I am artistic and crafty. That is not a separate side of me that switches on after work. It is the same part that cares whether a screen looks right.
- I am curious, and I like trying new things, even when I will be bad at them at first.
- I put a lot of effort into whatever I am doing, which has consequences, as you will read in a later post.
- I drink a lot of tea.
- I like being physically strong, and I keep track of it. Right now that means 50 push-ups and a 100 lb deadlift, which I am working on.

## What I am working on

The current mission, in the language of this site, is [ChainDeck](/projects/chaindeck), a live analytics dashboard for NIFTY options. Before that came [Unbake](/projects/unbake), which recovers spreadsheet formulas from plain values, and [GraphSentinel](/projects/graph-sentinel), an investigation agent for security alerts. They look unrelated, and the common thread is not an accident: all three take something opaque and make its structure visible.

## How I work

I like small, checkable steps. I like tests that can fail. I prefer a tool I can run on my own machine to one that needs an account. And I like writing things down, which is why this blog exists: explaining a project is the fastest way to find out whether I understood it.

## Why a blog with personal posts

Portfolios usually hide the person behind a list of skills. I would rather you could tell what I am like to work with, and what I care about when nobody is grading it. So alongside the technical posts there will be a few like this one.
