I like tea. This is not a quirk I put on a profile for effect. I like it a lot, and I drink a lot of it, and I have stopped apologising for the quantity.

## What I like about it

Tea is a good drink for anyone whose work is mostly sitting still and thinking, and I think it earns that reputation for reasons that have little to do with caffeine.

**It has a built-in pause.** You cannot rush a cup of tea. Water has to heat and leaves have to steep. Those few minutes are small enough not to feel wasteful and long enough to let a stuck problem loosen. A lot of my better ideas have not arrived at the keyboard.

**It is a task that is done when it is done.** Most of the things I work on do not have clean endings. A cup of tea does. You make it, you drink it, and the cup is empty. That small finished loop is oddly satisfying when everything else is still in progress.

**It is endlessly variable.** Strong or light, with milk or without, spiced or plain. There is always a new version to try, which suits someone who likes trying new things.

**It is easy to share.** Offering someone tea is one of the simplest friendly gestures I know, and it requires no explanation.

## The case against my own habit

Anything I drink in quantity deserves some honesty. Too much of it is too much, and I would be a poor advert for moderation. If I have been sitting for hours on tea alone, the sensible move is to stand up, drink some water and eat something. I know this. I am working on it.

## A small design lesson

I find it interesting how much a good habit looks like good software. It is a short loop with a clear start and end. It is hard to do badly. It is pleasant enough that you keep doing it. If I could make my tools as comfortable as a cup of tea, I would be happy.

Now, if you will excuse me, the kettle is on.
